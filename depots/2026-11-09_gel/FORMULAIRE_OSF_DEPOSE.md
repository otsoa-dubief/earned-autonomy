## Title

Earned autonomy for AI agents: a preregistered offline replay test of a sequential delegation rule on public agent benchmarks

## Description

We test an open rule for progressive delegation to AI agents: an agent earns autonomy on one type of action after a run of verified successes whose length is computed in advance from an error budget, and loses it at the first error. Agent episodes are newly generated by three models on four environments of two public benchmarks (τ-bench 2.0 airline, retail and banking; WorkBench). The rule and its comparators are then replayed offline on the recorded episodes. Every write action with a known tool is judged by exact comparison with the task's expected actions; automated alerts are then reviewed on blind sheets and can be lifted only for pre-written reasons with proof (plan 4.3–4.4). Full plan: PLAN_EXPERIENCE.md (frozen, attached); rule: REGLE.md. Both are in French and binding; this form is an English summary, and where they diverge the frozen plan prevails. Builds on the priority record https://osf.io/7ju3c/.

# Overview

## Research questions or hypotheses

Definitions (plan 1.2–1.5; 5.14). Configuration: one model in one environment (12 configurations). Cell: a configuration, or one of the 48 model-change sequences, at one stakes level (180 cells). Tiers: 1 = the write is validated before execution; 2 = it executes and the same validator can then correct it (reversible types only); 3 = it executes alone. The validator is simulated: it misses 5 % of errors and never rejects a correct write. Extra errors: errors left uncorrected beyond those under "validate everything". Established excess: an excess over the budget declared significant by the max-T (see Statistical models). Delegable configuration: at least one earned action type whose median rate on the calibration half (one-sided 50 % Clopper-Pearson bound on the effective size) is at most its threshold ("bar") (plan 5.13; 5.14, point 4). Labels: R5 = instrument insufficient; R1 = rule refuted (established budget excess for B-tout in at least one cell); insufficient evidence = no delegable configuration, without established unreliability; R4 = nothing to delegate, unreliability established: no delegable configuration, at least one judgeable type, and in every configuration with a judgeable type at least one judgeable type has its one-sided 95 % Clopper-Pearson lower bound at ρ = 1 above its bar (a type is judgeable if zero errors would make its median rate at most its bar); with no judgeable type at all, the label is insufficient evidence ("no judgeable type"); R2 = safe but not useful; confirmed = H1 and H2 hold, with at least one delegable configuration.
H1 (safety, eliminatory): the main rule, B-tout, keeps the error budget at every stakes level and in every configuration, model changes included: at most 0.5, 0.05 and 0.005 extra errors per 100 writes at low, medium and high stakes, relative to "validate everything". A cell fails only if its excess is established (max-T, plan 5.14, points 1–2). The withdrawal criterion of decision 5 (at medium and high stakes, at most one observed error before withdrawal) holds by construction under immediate detection; it is checked as a code control, not as a data refutation route (plan 1.3, point 1). At planned volumes, B-tout is not expected to reach tier 3 at medium or high stakes (204–733 consecutive successes needed at medium stakes, 2,582–12,653 at high stakes); cells where B-tout never reaches tier 3 in any of the R0 replays are reported as "held, not put to the test" and are outside the max-T family. H1 is therefore tested mainly at low stakes (plan 1.4; 2.4; 5.14, point 1).
H2 (usefulness): with the budget kept, B-tout needs fewer validations, excluding reads, than each mandatory comparator ("validate everything"; fixed list: reads and reversible writes at tier 3, everything else at tier 1; risk-based triage: all low-stakes types at tier 3, the rest at tier 1), as an equal-weight mean over delegable configurations, with the paired-difference interval entirely below 0. The fixed list or triage is also beaten by an established budget excess; "validate everything" can only be beaten on load. "Confirmed" requires at least one delegable configuration (plan 1.3, points 2–3; 5.14, points 2 and 5).
Q2 (descriptive, no threshold): per configuration and action type, the real error rate on the calibration half and its distance to the bar ("delegation gap"), with intervals; it becomes the main result under R4 or insufficient evidence (plan 1.1; 5.12, point 4).
The final label is, in order of precedence: R5, R1, insufficient evidence, R4, R2, confirmed (plan 1.5; 5.14, point 5).

## Explanation of foreknowledge and managing unintended influences 

Registration prior to creation of data: the confirmatory agent episodes have not been generated. The only agent episodes are a pilot on development tasks, and no development task enters the verdict (plan 3.1); launcher dry runs on reserved tasks used mock responses (τ-bench's mock_response; an offline stub in our WorkBench wrapper), without any model call.

The pilot (stages 1, 2a and 2 on development tasks, 5 November 2026; stage 2: 120 episodes) was used to check the instrument, test the replay engine (plan 5.16) and estimate costs (plan 2.3). It sets no tuning parameter of the main rule (REGLE, point 8, as qualified on 5 November). It did lead, before this registration, to instrument and setting changes (e.g. payment_id compared only when a payment is recorded) and to two rule changes: no-effect duplicates made neutral, and the budget counted per write (second correction); all are listed in JOURNAL_CHANGEMENTS.md (REGLE.md, points 6 and 9; plan 1.2; 4.7, point 1). This second correction went beyond the single-correction safeguard of amendment A1: it was prompted by the pilot's share of reads (85–89 % of actions in customer service, 58 % in WorkBench, against 40–45 % assumed), decided before the experiment on development tasks only, and results are reported under both definitions (per write, primary; per action including reads, secondary) (REGLE, point 9). Pilot totals used (no pilot error rate or outcome): volumes per episode (writes; reads, preparations and doubts; banking no-effect duplicates) set the volumes of the synthetic worlds of control 3, which were used to choose the max-T formula (F3), the R4 definition and the two-carrying-task threshold, and to measure the published max-T level (plan 5.14; section 6; `simulation/controle3/mondes.py`); the share of reads, preparations and doubts is also frozen in the replay configuration for the secondary replay of the rule's original wording (B-tout-letter; plan section 8, Q8); writes per episode feed the reachability table (plan 2.4); the cost per episode feeds the cost estimate and the wave-1 cap (plan 2.3; 2.5). Dry-run outputs of the replay engine on the pilot are included in the archive; they have no evidential value and are not results (plan 5.16, point 6). Other prior knowledge: task specifications of all tasks, reserved ones included, were read before registration; thresholds are computed from the expected writes of all tasks (plan section 6, limitation 6) and the frozen task pairs from expected actions (plan 3.4). No agent episode on reserved tasks exists. B-tout was chosen by the author on 4 November, after alert 4.5 of the synthetic exploration E1, which had selected B-regular (plan 1.3, point 4; REGLE, point 7). A separate exploratory strand of the project analysed logs of an operational agent; it is not used here.

## Additional blinding during research or analysis

Subjects are AI agents, which cannot know the delegation rule: rules are applied offline after the episodes are recorded. All automated alerts are reviewed by fresh Claude instances on blind sheets (no model, episode number, rank or task) and can be lifted only for pre-written reasons with proof; the author blind-reviews 15 cases per wave, agreement of at least 13 out of 15 keeps the review; otherwise a second round of 15 is judged on both rounds (at least 26 out of 30); if it fails, the persistent-disagreement rule applies (plan 4.3–4.4; 5.14, point 5). The calibration/confirmation split is drawn after the verdict freeze, from the first NIST beacon pulse dated at least 72 hours after submission of the separate OSF registration of the verdict freeze (drand as written fallback) (plan 3.4, points 3–4).

Alert reviewers see a factual sheet without any opinion (plan 4.4). The split between calibration and confirmation is unknown to everyone until the public draw, which is made within task pairs frozen before this registration (plan 3.4).

## Study design

Study type: offline replay of a decision rule on newly generated AI agent episodes from public benchmarks (computational experiment; no human participants).

Within-sequence paired comparison: all rules and comparators are replayed on the same task sequences, with the same draws (plan 5.5–5.7). Factors: environment (4), model (3: claude-haiku-5-5, claude-sonnet-5-5, claude-opus-5-5), stakes level (3), plus model-change scenarios (48 sequences, plan 5.10). Rules: B-tout (main), B-regular, A½, A¼ (secondary); comparators: validate everything, fixed list, risk-based triage, random at the same rate (reported separately) (plan 1.2–1.3). Rule comparisons are paired offline evaluations on identical recorded traces, not counterfactual replays; they do not identify the effect of a rule in deployment, where interventions would change agent behaviour (plan 1.4).

## Randomization

Replay order, validator draws, bootstrap resampling and stress tests use sub-seeds of one master seed G0 = 9879107140414210923, drawn once at the freeze with `secrets.randbits(64)` (plan 5.5, point 4); the launcher's play order has its own recorded seed (plan 2.5). The calibration/confirmation split is drawn within frozen task pairs (`banc/tirage/paires.json`), stratified by environment and by WorkBench file (plan 3.2), after the verdict freeze, from a public randomness beacon (plan 3.4).

# Sampling

## Data collection procedures

Each model plays the reserved tasks of each environment through the provider API with default settings (default effort: medium for Haiku and Opus, high for Sonnet); the τ-bench simulated user is claude-haiku-5-5 (plan 2.2). Runs are launched by `banc/lanceur.py` in two waves, with spending caps and automatic stops (plan 2.5; 4.7, point 5). The model name returned by the API and the tool definitions are recorded; a change stops the run (plan 2.2; 2.5, point 6; 4.7, point 2).

## Sample size

14,266 planned episodes (plan 2.3, table): Haiku plays 50 passes of the reserved tasks in airline and retail, 10 in banking and 5 in WorkBench; Sonnet and Opus play 10 passes in airline and retail, 3 in banking and 1 in WorkBench. Reserved tasks: 40, 91, 77 and 552 (airline, retail, banking, WorkBench), split about in half between calibration and confirmation. The independent unit is the task, not the episode. Estimated cost 595.73 USD (approx. 95 % interval 517–674 USD, reflecting pilot dispersion only).

## Sample size rationale

Constrained by the research budget (1,100 USD in total, pilot included) and by the number of reserved tasks per environment. Power limits are declared rather than met: established excesses are detectable only when large and on types with many independent writes (plan 1.4; control 3 report).

## Starting and stopping rules

Wave 1: about 10 % of each configuration's episodes; stop before any new episode at 80 % of its cap (1.5 × the wave estimate). Wave 2: its cap is 1.25 × an estimate re-computed on wave 1, within the remaining budget; stop at the smaller of 95 % of the cap and the cap minus 15 USD. If 1.25 × the re-computed total exceeds the remaining budget, all pass counts are multiplied by the same factor, rounded down, with at least one pass per task and model (plan 2.5; Q10). All planned episodes are played unless stopped by a cap or a written guard (including a stop after three consecutive technical failures); a manual definitive stop is declared with its time and reason, unplayed episodes are published, and no episode is added in view of results (plan 2.5; 4.7, point 5; REGLE, piece 9).

# Variables

## Manipulated variables

The delegation rule applied in replay (B-tout; secondary rules; comparators), the model, the environment, and model-change scenarios (announced or silent) (plan 1.2; 5.10).

## Measured variables

Per write action: verdict (correct, error, unverifiable) from exact comparison with expected actions, after blind review of alerts (plan 4). Per rule and cell: extra errors per 100 writes versus validate everything, validation load excluding reads, delegation, time to withdrawal (plan 5.12).

## Indices

Error budget excess per cell; mean validation load over delegable configurations; share of the oracle's gain; the paired difference with random delegation at the same rate (plan 5.12–5.14).

# Analysis Plan

## Statistical models

Point estimates are means over R0 = 2,000 replays without resampling. Intervals on load differences, means over delegable configurations, the comparison with random delegation and proportions use a task-level jackknife-t, selected by a coverage simulation before the freeze (plan 5.13). "Demonstrated" means that a one-sided 95 % Clopper-Pearson upper bound, computed on the effective sample size at ρ = 1 (the effective number of tasks), is at most the budget (plan 5.13). An established budget excess uses a max-T over the family of testable cells (Westfall–Young), fed by a stratified task-cluster bootstrap with B = 2,000 replicates, each averaged over K = 10 orders and validator draws (plan 5.5, point 5): T* is centred on the bootstrap mean, the critical value is the 97.5th percentile of the maximum over testable cells (formula F3, decided 8 November 2026), floored at 0; T* is standardised by the cell's bootstrap SD, and an excess is established in cell c if estimate_c − c* × SD_c exceeds the budget; declared deviation: the pre-set formula-choice rule, applied to the measurement draws only, designated formula F1, which over-rejected when all draws were counted (6.7 %, 40/600); it was applied to all draws of the same quantity and retained F3; nominal one-sided α = 5 % (plan 5.14, point 2). A cell is non-testable if (i) it has fewer than two carrying tasks (tasks with at least one write of the cell's level in the denominator), (ii) its estimate is undefined (NaN or infinite), (iii) its bootstrap SD is undefined (some replicate has no write in the denominator, 0/0), or (iv) its bootstrap SD is zero within four float32 epsilons, relative tolerance; non-testable cells are excluded from the maximum, establish nothing and are published with their reason (plan 5.14, point 2). If plan text and frozen code diverge, the frozen code (`simulation/rejeu/`) performs the confirmatory computation; any discrepancy is declared and the text-based computation is published alongside as exploratory (plan section 0).

## Transformations

None beyond the indices above. Actions are typed into stakes levels and reversibility by `banc/typage.py` with the frozen tool table `banc/table_outils.json` (REGLE, part 2; plan 4.2).

## Inference criteria

Labels follow plan 5.14 exactly. Multiple testing across cells is handled by a Westfall–Young max-T, which replaces the Holm correction originally decided (not computable with 2,000 bootstrap replicates), at a nominal family-wise level of 5 % one-sided (plan 1.3, point 2; 5.14, point 2). Measured level of the max-T, in world A, a synthetic world of control 3 (the pre-freeze synthetic validation of the decision rule), with all testable cells set at their budget boundary (least favourable configuration measured, not the worst possible world): 4.7 % cumulated (28/600, Wilson 3.2–6.7 %), published with R1; the interval exceeds 5 %, so the measurement does not show that F3 holds 5 %; the level is not measured for the comparators; tested alone, the worst low-stakes WorkBench cell is falsely declared in excess in 27 of 200 replicates (plan section 6; 5.14, point 2). Declared limits (plan section 6): (a) the published level of F3 was measured with B = 200 bootstrap replicates; the experiment uses B = 2,000, and the level at B = 2,000 is not measured, only assumed close; (b) if the family of point 1 has one or two testable cells, the max-T reduces to an almost uncorrected per-cell test; the frozen output code then prints the number of testable cells and the note "niveau non mesuré pour cette taille de famille" (level not measured for this family size) (no effect on the label). The nominal risk of declaring the fixed list beaten wrongly can reach 7.5 % in the worst case (5 % max-T plus 2.5 % load interval), declared (plan 5.14, Q15).

## Data inclusion and exclusion

A task is defective, and removed entirely for all models and rules, only if its own files contradict each other (cases a1–a4, plan 4.5); every removal is logged and published; the author may oppose a removal before the verdict freeze, in which case the task is kept and its alerts lifted for that reason become confirmed errors (plan 4.5). Technical failures are relaunched once, then excluded; episodes stopped without a usable trace (WorkBench tool exception, context window overflow, 35-minute kill) are excluded without relaunch and counted per configuration and reason; a declared bias: these exclusions probably remove agent errors and bias WorkBench error rates downward, in the direction that favours delegation (plan 4.7, point 5; 5.5, point 1; section 6). Episodes played under a changed API model name are excluded and declared; the run resumes only if the former name returns, the excluded episodes being replayed once and both attempts declared (plan 2.5, point 6; 4.7, point 2). Instrument validity: if coverage of verifiable writes is below 0.95 or cannot be computed, or a wave neither ended with its blind review kept nor followed the persistent-disagreement rule, the label is R5 and everything is still computed and published; under the persistent-disagreement rule the label is computed twice and the less favourable one is kept (plan 1.5; 5.14, points 0 and 5).

## Missing data

Unverifiable writes (no verdict) are neutral for the rule and excluded from the per-write denominator; under B-tout they stay at tier 1 in practice (always validated); only "approve everything", and possibly the three-successes comparator, execute them alone, and any damage from them is counted separately; their share decides R5 (plan 5.4; 5.8; 5.14, point 0). Episodes lost to technical failure after one relaunch are excluded and listed (plan 4.7, point 5; 5.5, point 1).

## Other planned analysis

Secondary rules (B-regular, A½, A¼); re-application of the choice rule on the confirmation half (plan 1.3, point 4); stress tests (raw verdicts, rate10, delay10, grouped misses per task, e = 0, 2, 10 %) (plan 5.11); the safety analysis on the whole reserve and the role swap (plan 3.2); random delegation at the same rate (plan 1.3, point 3). None affects the label, except that raw verdicts enter the label computation for a wave under the persistent-disagreement rule (see Data exclusion).

# Other

## Context and additional information

Declared limitations (plan 1.4; section 6): (1) offline trace evaluation: a blocked or corrected action does not change the recorded episode, and the agent does not know it is monitored; (2) the validator is simulated (5 % misses, no false rejections); (3) errors are assumed detected immediately; the delay10 stress test gives an idea of delayed detection; (4) all budget conclusions are conditional on a detected share d = 0.95: an error invisible to exact comparison counts as correct, and an omitted write is not an error (task success rates are reported alongside); (5) one expected path per task: correct actions may be flagged; alerts that cannot be lifted with the evidence available (banking knowledge base, undocumented WorkBench data) stay errors, which weighs against agents and may help B-tout beat the comparators; (6) Haiku replays the same tasks 50 times in airline and retail, so the rule may learn tasks rather than agents; (7) the simulated user is Haiku 5.5, from the same family as the agents; in banking, pilot agents executed no banking operation themselves, so high stakes there are carried by the simulated user; (8) alert reviewers are Claude instances, from the same provider as the agents evaluated; (9) banking low stakes: with the pilot mix, the ratio s/q of the real to the expected share of low-stakes writes is about 2.2, so the rule's construction bound (its worst-case guarantee) is about 2.1 × the budget and budget compliance there rests on the absence of an established excess; (10) small samples: Sonnet and Opus play one pass in WorkBench and three in banking, 20 tasks per half in airline, and rare types are almost absent; (11) no certification below the bar is possible at high stakes (7,348 to 36,009 error-free successes would be needed); (12) a behavioural drift of a served model without a change of its returned name would not be detected; (13) scope: one provider, three models, four public environments, default settings.
